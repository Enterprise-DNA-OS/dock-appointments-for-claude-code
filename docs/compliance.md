# Dock evidence checks

Checked 10 November 2026. These are record checks for an operator, not safety certification or legal advice. A completed record cannot establish that a dock, vehicle or loading activity is safe.

[WorkSafe NZ: managing work site traffic](https://www.worksafe.govt.nz/topic-and-industry/vehicles-and-mobile-plant/site-traffic-management/managing-work-site-traffic-gpg/) covers shared traffic risks, load information, a loading-zone controller, separation, vehicle restraints and driver instructions. Sections 5.5, 5.6 and 8 inform the evidence fields. The software checks that observations were recorded; it cannot inspect the physical controls.

[Safe Work Australia: traffic management risks](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/managing-health-and-safety/traffic-management/managing-risks) describes identifying and controlling traffic risks with workers. Consult the site's jurisdiction and competent adviser for its applicable duties.

| Rule | Implemented check | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| TRAFFIC-PLAN | Site has a plan reference and an unexpired review date | Operator policy inspired by the guidance, not a statutory review interval |
| LOAD-EVIDENCE | In-progress or completed load has a safety observation | Evidence completeness only |
| TIMELINE | Arrival, loading and completion observations exist for the status | Internal data integrity rule |
| DEPARTURE | Completed load has a recorded departure | Internal completeness rule |

Start requires a prior safety check with named controller, load-information evidence, restraint evidence, exclusion-zone evidence and driver briefing. The operator sets the traffic-plan review date. No statute is represented as mandating the example review interval, 120 free minutes or 15-minute lateness threshold. Detention is elapsed time for review against the actual contract, never an automatically payable amount. Preserve history and follow your business's retention and access policy.
